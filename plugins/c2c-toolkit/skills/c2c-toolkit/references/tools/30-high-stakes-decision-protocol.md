# THE HIGH-STAKES DECISION PROTOCOL
Author: Eric Weiss  
Purpose: Force Clarity and Decisiveness on Irreversible or Identity-Level Decisions  
Audience: Founder / CEO  

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS TOOL

Certain decisions create emotional paralysis:

- Fire / don’t fire
- Raise / don’t raise
- Pivot / don’t pivot
- Replace exec / coach longer
- Shut down product line / double down
- Step down / stay CEO
- Sell company / keep building

These decisions are not informationally blocked.

They are emotionally blocked.

Common founder patterns:

- Seeking more data endlessly
- Polling too many opinions
- Delaying until forced
- Hoping situation resolves itself
- Avoiding short-term discomfort

The High-Stakes Decision Protocol forces clarity under pressure.

---

# 2. WHEN TO USE

Use when:

- Decision has material consequences
- Decision delayed multiple weeks
- Emotional charge high
- Team waiting for clarity
- Board pressing
- Founder ruminating repeatedly
- Status or identity implicated

This is not for small tradeoffs.

---

# 3. STEP 1 — DEFINE THE ACTUAL DECISION

State clearly:

The decision I am making is ________.

Example:

“Whether to replace my CTO.”

Not:

“How do we improve engineering?”

Name the binary or primary fork.

---

# 4. STEP 2 — REMOVE THE NOISE

Ask:

If I had to decide today with current information, what would I choose?

Most founders already know.

Capture the instinctive answer.

Then ask:

What is stopping me from executing that?

Surface fear.

---

# 5. STEP 3 — DEFINE THE FEAR

Common fears:

- Looking wrong
- Damaging reputation
- Losing team trust
- Investor reaction
- Regret
- Public perception
- Short-term revenue hit
- Being perceived as unstable

Write the fear explicitly.

Clarity reduces its power.

---

# 6. STEP 4 — CONSEQUENCE MATRIX

For each option define:

Option A:
- Short-term cost
- Long-term cost
- Short-term benefit
- Long-term benefit

Option B:
- Short-term cost
- Long-term cost
- Short-term benefit
- Long-term benefit

Force honesty.

Avoid fantasy upside.

---

# 7. STEP 5 — REVERSIBILITY TEST

Ask:

Is this reversible?

If reversible:
Lower emotional weight.

If irreversible:
Increase decision discipline.

Not all big decisions are permanent.

---

# 8. STEP 6 — 12-MONTH LOOKBACK TEST

Imagine 12 months from now.

Ask:

Which decision would I regret not making?

Often clarity appears.

Time perspective removes ego.

---

# 9. STEP 7 — IDENTITY CHECK

Ask:

Which decision aligns with the leader I am becoming?

Connect to Identity Ceiling Tool if needed.

Short-term comfort often conflicts with long-term identity.

---

# 10. STEP 8 — DECISION DEADLINE

Set explicit deadline:

Within 48–72 hours for most cases.

Indefinite deliberation is avoidance.

Commit to execution window.

---

# 11. STEP 9 — EXECUTION PLAN

After decision, define:

- Who must be informed?
- What is the script?
- What timeline?
- What risk mitigation?
- What metrics to monitor?

Decisions without execution are illusions.

---

# 12. FAILURE MODES

AI must guard against:

- Endless analysis
- Seeking certainty
- Waiting for perfect data
- Outsourcing responsibility
- Blaming external conditions
- Emotional rationalization
- Not setting deadline

Speed under clarity builds authority.

---

# 13. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES

If another AI runs this protocol:

It must:

1. Define decision clearly.
2. Surface instinctive answer.
3. Identify fear explicitly.
4. Build consequence matrix.
5. Test reversibility.
6. Run 12-month lookback.
7. Align with identity.
8. Set deadline.
9. Define execution plan.

Tone:

- Calm
- Direct
- Grounded
- Decisive
- Slightly firm against avoidance

Never:

- Offer platitudes.
- Encourage over-analysis.
- Suggest polling endlessly.
- Avoid deadline enforcement.
