# DOMINO STRATEGY EXERCISE
Author: Eric Weiss  
Purpose: Sequential Capability Design for Scalable Execution  
Audience: Growth-Stage Founders and Executive Teams  

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS FRAMEWORK

Most founders attempt to pursue multiple strategic initiatives simultaneously.

They hire before clarity.
They expand before stabilization.
They fundraise before operational maturity.

This creates strategic thrashing.

The Domino Strategy Exercise forces founders to:

- Sequence growth intentionally
- Build capabilities before scale
- Reduce risk compounding
- Avoid parallel chaos
- Turn ambition into engineered progression

This is about leverage stacking.

---

# 2. CORE ANALOGY

Each strategic milestone is a domino.

When it falls, it:

- Builds capability
- Reduces uncertainty
- Increases leverage
- Makes the next domino easier

If dominos are placed randomly:

- Effort increases
- Risk compounds
- Leadership stress spikes
- Execution fragments

The goal is to create a chain reaction.

---

# 3. WHEN TO USE THIS

Use after completing:

- Time Machine Vision Exercise
- Strategic clarity session
- Post-fundraise reset
- Growth plateau diagnosis

Do not use during:
- Immediate crisis
- Tactical firefighting weeks
- Team restructuring chaos

This requires strategic perspective.

---

# 4. STEP 1 — DEFINE THE FINAL DOMINO

Start with:

"What must be undeniably true three years from now?"

Examples:

- $100M ARR with 30% margin
- Category leadership in specific niche
- Fully delegated executive team
- Profitable and independent

Force precision.

Ask:

- What measurable proof exists?
- What capability makes this sustainable?
- What must no longer rely on you?

Document clearly.

---

# 5. STEP 2 — IDENTIFY REQUIRED CAPABILITIES

Work backward from final state.

For each element of the future state, ask:

- What capability enables this?
- What system must exist?
- What team strength must be in place?
- What constraint must be eliminated?

Examples:

Final State: $100M ARR  
Required Capabilities:
- Repeatable enterprise sales motion
- Predictable pipeline generation
- Scalable onboarding system
- Mature engineering velocity
- Delegated decision authority

---

# 6. STEP 3 — ORDER THE DOMINOS

Now sequence them.

Criteria for sequencing:

1. Risk Reduction
2. Capability Building
3. Revenue Unlock
4. Founder Bottleneck Removal
5. Structural Leverage

Ask:

- Which capability unlocks the most downstream impact?
- Which reduces existential risk?
- Which removes dependency on the founder?
- Which improves data visibility?

Domino ordering rule:

Never build demand before operational capacity.
Never hire before authority clarity.
Never scale before system stability.

---

# 7. STEP 4 — DEFINE CLEAR MILESTONES

Each domino must have:

- Clear success criteria
- Measurable proof
- Time boundary
- Owner

Template:

| Domino | Capability | Success Metric | Owner | Timeframe |
|--------|------------|---------------|-------|-----------|

Example:

| Build repeatable sales playbook | Documented sales motion | 3 consecutive months >70% close rate in ICP | CRO | Q2 |

If success is vague, the domino is invalid.

---

# 8. STEP 5 — IDENTIFY PARALLELIZATION RISKS

Founders tend to:

- Attempt 3 dominos at once
- Launch initiatives prematurely
- Add complexity before stability

The AI must ask:

- What are you trying to do simultaneously?
- Which initiative is ego-driven?
- Which domino would collapse others if it fails?
- What are you tolerating that blocks this domino?

Force tradeoffs.

---

# 9. STEP 6 — ALIGN ORGANIZATION TO DOMINO

Once next domino is chosen:

- Company objectives align to it
- Hiring aligns to it
- Resource allocation aligns to it
- KPIs align to it

No side quests.

The entire organization must feel gravitational pull toward the active domino.

---

# 10. VISUALIZATION STRUCTURE

Encourage founders to visualize:

Domino 1 → Domino 2 → Domino 3 → Domino 4 → Final State

Not as a list.

As a compounding leverage chain.

Each domino should increase the size and power of the next.

---

# 11. FAILURE MODES

AI must guard against:

1. Ambition stacking
Founder adds too many dominos.

2. Vanity sequencing
Dominos ordered for optics, not leverage.

3. Fear avoidance
Founder delays structural authority domino.

4. Tactical confusion
Founder confuses tasks with capabilities.

5. Hiring as solution
Founder substitutes hiring for system design.

---

# 12. DOMINO TYPES

Common categories:

Revenue Dominos:
- ICP clarity
- Sales playbook
- Channel expansion

Product Dominos:
- Platform stability
- Architecture refactor
- Core feature maturity

Operational Dominos:
- KPI dashboard implementation
- Escalation protocol clarity
- Authority redesign

Leadership Dominos:
- Delegation commitment
- Executive alignment
- Performance management discipline

Emotional Dominos:
- Letting go of control
- Having hard conversation
- Ending underperformer tenure

Often the emotional domino unlocks operational domino.

---

# 13. IMPLEMENTATION CADENCE

Weekly:

- Review active domino
- Identify friction
- Remove bottleneck

Monthly:

- Reassess sequence
- Confirm resource alignment

Quarterly:

- Declare domino complete
- Activate next domino

No domino remains active beyond defined timeframe.

---

# 14. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES

If another AI uses this framework:

It must:

1. Refuse vague final states.
2. Force measurable capability definitions.
3. Challenge simultaneous execution.
4. Tie every domino to leverage.
5. Explicitly identify emotional avoidance.
6. End with one active domino commitment.

Tone:

- Strategic
- Uncompromising
- Calm
- Systems-oriented
- Execution-focused

Never:

- Allow motivational fluff
- Accept multi-directional strategy
- Permit undefined ownership
- Allow infinite horizon goals
