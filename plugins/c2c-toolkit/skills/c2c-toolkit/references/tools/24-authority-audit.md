# THE AUTHORITY AUDIT
Author: Eric Weiss  
Purpose: Eliminate Political Drift and Restore Clean Decision Rights  
Audience: Founder / Executive Team  

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS TOOL

As companies grow, authority blurs.

Symptoms appear:

- Decisions revisited repeatedly
- Escalations increase
- Meetings multiply
- Founder overrides quietly
- Executives hesitate
- Political behavior emerges
- Accountability weakens

Most teams assume:

“We need better communication.”

In reality:

Authority is unclear.

The Authority Audit exposes invisible vetoes, shared ownership illusions, and escalation ambiguity.

It restores clean decision rights.

---

# 2. WHEN TO USE

Use when:

- CEO feels overloaded with approvals
- CTO/COO friction appears
- Same decisions debated repeatedly
- Leaders complain about being overruled
- Execution slows
- Executive team appears cautious
- Founder says “Why do I have to decide everything?”

Authority confusion compounds at scale.

---

# 3. STEP 1 — DECISION INVENTORY

List major decision categories from the last 60 days:

Examples:

- Pricing
- Hiring
- Roadmap
- Architecture
- Budget allocation
- Sales discounting
- Marketing positioning
- Vendor selection
- Customer escalations

Write them explicitly.

---

# 4. STEP 2 — CURRENT DECISION FLOW

For each category, document:

- Who proposes?
- Who decides?
- Who can veto?
- Who must be consulted?
- Who is informed?

Often the “real” decision maker differs from the official one.

Expose reality, not org chart theory.

---

# 5. STEP 3 — OVERRIDE DETECTION

Ask:

In the last 90 days:

- How often did the founder override?
- How often were decisions reopened?
- How often were decisions escalated unnecessarily?
- Where did leaders hesitate to act?

Overrides erode authority silently.

If overrides frequent:
Authority not truly delegated.

---

# 6. STEP 4 — SHARED OWNERSHIP EXPOSURE

Look for phrases like:

- “We decided…”
- “Let’s align…”
- “Joint call…”
- “Let’s discuss further…”

Shared ownership without single accountability creates paralysis.

For each decision category ask:

Is there one final accountable decision owner?

If no:
Authority leak detected.

---

# 7. STEP 5 — ESCALATION THRESHOLD CLARITY

For each decision category define:

- What is fully owned at level X?
- What dollar threshold triggers escalation?
- What risk level triggers escalation?
- What timeline triggers escalation?
- What requires board input?

If thresholds unclear:
Escalations become political.

---

# 8. STEP 6 — INVISIBLE VETO MAPPING

Ask:

Who informally influences decisions after they are made?

Examples:

- Founder post-meeting edits
- Board member private messages
- Senior IC undermining exec
- Advisor opinion overrides
- Culture hero influencing roadmap

Invisible veto power destroys executive confidence.

Expose it directly.

---

# 9. STEP 7 — AUTHORITY RESET DESIGN

For each decision category define:

| Decision Category | Final Owner | Escalation Rule | Board Involvement | Review Cadence |

Clarify publicly.

Commit to non-interference within boundaries.

Authority must be clean.

---

# 10. STEP 8 — PUBLIC DECLARATION

Authority resets must be visible.

Founder states:

“These decisions are fully owned by X within these thresholds.”

Without public declaration:
Political drift continues.

---

Rate 1–5:

| Dimension | Score |
|------------|-------|
| Decision clarity |
| Escalation clarity |

Any category below 3 requires redesign.

---

# 12. FAILURE MODES

AI must guard against:

- Avoiding founder interference discussion
- Protecting egos
- Leaving invisible veto unaddressed
- Redesigning without public reset
- Allowing shared ownership to persist
- Suggesting more meetings as solution

Authority clarity reduces meetings.

---

# 13. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES

If another AI runs this tool:

It must:

1. Create decision inventory.
2. Map real decision flow.
3. Detect override frequency.
4. Expose shared ownership.
5. Clarify escalation thresholds.
6. Surface invisible vetoes.
7. Redesign authority matrix.
8. End with public declaration script.

Tone:

- Direct
- Structured
- Calm
- Slightly uncomfortable where necessary
- Systems-focused

Never:

- Avoid political realities.
- Assume org chart equals authority.
- Suggest vague alignment exercises.
- Skip founder accountability.
