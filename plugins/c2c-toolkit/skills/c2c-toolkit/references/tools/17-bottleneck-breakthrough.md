# 30-MINUTE BOTTLENECK BREAKTHROUGH
Author: Eric Weiss  
Purpose: Rapid Constraint Identification and Structural Redesign  
Audience: Growth-Stage Founder  

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS TOOL

When a founder says:

- “Everything feels stuck.”
- “We’re not moving fast enough.”
- “My team is underperforming.”
- “Growth has slowed.”

The instinct is to solve multiple problems.

That is wrong.

At any given moment, one constraint limits the next level of scale.

This tool isolates it.
Redesigns it.
Forces action.

It is surgical.

---

# 2. WHEN TO USE

Use when:

- Growth plateaus
- Execution slows
- Founder overwhelmed
- Cross-functional tension rises
- Revenue unpredictable
- Meetings increase but output does not

Do not use for minor tactical issues.

This is for structural friction.

---

# 3. THE CORE QUESTION

If we could fix only ONE thing this month that would unlock the most leverage, what would it be?

Do not allow multiple answers.

Force compression.

---

# 4. STEP 1 — DEFINE THE SYMPTOM

Ask:

- What is visibly not working?
- Where is output below expectation?
- What frustrates you most right now?
- What keeps coming up in meetings?
- Where is energy lowest?

Write the symptom clearly.

Example:

“Sales pipeline inconsistent.”

“Engineering velocity slowing.”

“Too many escalations.”

---

# 5. STEP 2 — CLASSIFY THE CONSTRAINT

Force classification:

Is this primarily:

- Demand constraint
- Capacity constraint
- Authority constraint
- Clarity constraint
- Skill constraint
- System constraint
- Emotional constraint

If founder hesitates, probe.

Example:

Pipeline inconsistent → Often clarity or ICP constraint, not sales skill.

Escalations frequent → Authority constraint.

Slow shipping → System or architectural constraint.

Classification matters.

---

# 6. STEP 3 — IDENTIFY THE HUMAN BOTTLENECK

Ask:

Who must approve most things?
Who is overloaded?
Where does work queue?
Whose calendar is the constraint?

Then ask:

If this person disappeared for 30 days, what would break?

If answer is “everything,” that’s the bottleneck.

Often it is the founder.

---

# 7. STEP 4 — SURFACE THE AVOIDANCE

Now the hard part.

Ask:

What conversation are you avoiding?
What authority are you not transferring?
What decision are you delaying?
What standard are you not enforcing?
What fear keeps this in place?

This step prevents superficial redesign.

---

# 8. STEP 5 — STRUCTURAL REDESIGN

Redesign must include one of:

- Authority shift
- Decision threshold clarity
- KPI visibility upgrade
- Interface documentation
- Role redefinition
- Meeting elimination
- Escalation rule definition
- Performance reset

Ask:

What structural move reduces this bottleneck permanently?

Not a temporary fix.
A system change.

---

# 9. STEP 6 — TIME-BOUND COMMITMENT

Define:

- Exact action
- Owner
- Date
- Observable proof of completion

Example:

“By Tuesday, CRO owns pricing decisions under $50K without CEO approval.”

“Engineering lead publishes deployment SLA by Friday.”

“Founder stops reviewing minor PRs immediately.”

No vague commitments.

---

# 10. COMMON FAILURE MODES

AI must guard against:

- Identifying multiple bottlenecks
- Treating symptom instead of constraint
- Blaming individuals
- Avoiding authority shift
- Suggesting hiring prematurely
- Ending without deadline

The power is in compression.

---

# 11. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES

If another AI runs this tool:

It must:

1. Force a single bottleneck.
2. Classify the constraint type.
3. Identify human dependency.
4. Surface emotional avoidance.
5. Propose structural redesign.
6. Demand time-bound commitment.
7. Provide concise summary.

Tone:

- Direct
- Calm
- Slightly impatient with vagueness
- Systems-focused
- Action-oriented

Never:

- Accept “it’s complicated.”
- Allow multiple simultaneous focuses.
- Recommend generic “better communication.”
- Leave without commitment.
