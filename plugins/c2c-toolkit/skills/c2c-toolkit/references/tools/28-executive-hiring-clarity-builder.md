# THE EXECUTIVE HIRING CLARITY BUILDER
Author: Eric Weiss  
Purpose: Define Outcome-Based Executive Roles Before Hiring  
Audience: Founder / CEO  

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS TOOL

Founders often hire executives based on:

- Resume prestige
- Brand logos
- Urgency
- Investor pressure
- Personality fit
- Hope

They do not hire against a clear mandate.

Common outcomes:

- Misaligned expectations
- Authority confusion
- Slow ramp
- Founder interference
- Early churn
- Expensive reset

The Executive Hiring Clarity Builder forces:

- Outcome-based role definition
- 12-month mandate clarity
- Authority boundaries
- Explicit success and failure conditions

Clarity prevents political drift.

---

# 2. WHEN TO USE

Use when:

- Hiring VP-level or above
- Promoting internal leader to executive role
- Replacing underperforming exec
- Expanding into new function
- Founder transitioning responsibility

Do not hire before completing this.

---

# 3. STEP 1 — DEFINE THE REAL PROBLEM

Ask:

What structural constraint is this hire meant to remove?

Examples:

- Pipeline unpredictability
- Engineering quality variance
- Product-market ambiguity
- Hiring chaos
- Revenue plateau
- Financial opacity
- Founder bottleneck

If problem unclear, role will drift.

---

# 4. STEP 2 — 12-MONTH OUTCOME MANDATE

Define:

If this hire is wildly successful in 12 months, what is undeniably true?

Force specificity:

- Revenue number
- KPI movement
- Team size and quality
- Process maturity
- Delegation clarity
- Reduced founder involvement

Example:

“Pipeline predictability within ±10% variance monthly.”

“Deployment frequency weekly with <1% rollback rate.”

“ICP documented and enforced.”

Vague outcomes produce vague performance.

---

# 5. STEP 3 — AUTHORITY BOUNDARY DEFINITION

Clarify:

What decisions are fully owned by this role?

Examples:

- Pricing thresholds
- Roadmap prioritization
- Hiring decisions
- Vendor selection
- Budget allocation

Also clarify:

What decisions remain founder-owned?

Authority must be explicit before hire.

---

# 6. STEP 4 — NON-NEGOTIABLE STANDARDS

Define:

What behaviors are mandatory?

Examples:

- Clear written updates weekly
- KPI transparency
- Decisive action
- No escalation without solution proposal
- Cultural leadership
- Ownership mentality

Write standards clearly.

---

# 7. STEP 5 — FAILURE CONDITIONS

Ask:

What would make this hire clearly unsuccessful?

Examples:

- Missed KPIs without explanation
- Blaming other departments
- Authority avoidance
- Poor hiring discipline
- No process improvement
- Repeated escalation upward

Define failure early to avoid ambiguity.

---

# 8. STEP 6 — INTERVIEW ALIGNMENT

Convert mandate into interview filters:

Ask candidates:

- How have you solved X before?
- What system did you design?
- How did you define authority?
- How did you handle underperformance?
- What was your 12-month measurable impact?

Score against mandate, not charisma.

---

# 9. STEP 7 — 30-60-90 PLAN FRAME

Before offer accepted:

Require candidate to draft:

- 30-day diagnostic focus
- 60-day structural changes
- 90-day visible progress markers

Alignment before start prevents friction.

---

# 10. FOUNDER READINESS CHECK

Ask founder:

Are you prepared to let this person own their domain fully?

If hesitation:

Resolve before hiring.

Authority confusion kills executive performance.

---

# 11. FAILURE MODES

AI must guard against:

- Vague job descriptions
- Hiring for comfort
- Ignoring authority clarity
- Undefined success metrics
- No failure definition
- Founder unwilling to release control
- Emotional hiring decisions

Clarity prevents regret.

---

# 12. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES

If another AI runs this tool:

It must:

1. Identify structural constraint.
2. Define 12-month outcome.
3. Clarify authority boundaries.
4. Define non-negotiable standards.
5. Define failure conditions.
6. Convert into interview questions.
7. Require 30-60-90 alignment.
8. Confirm founder readiness.

Tone:

- Structured
- Direct
- Outcome-focused
- Strategic
- Slightly confronting if founder hesitant

Never:

- Allow vague mandate.
- Ignore authority discussion.
- Skip failure clarity.
- Treat hiring as personality match exercise.
