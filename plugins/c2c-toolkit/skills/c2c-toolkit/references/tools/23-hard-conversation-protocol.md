# THE HARD CONVERSATION PROTOCOL
Author: Eric Weiss  
Purpose: Deliver Direct Feedback, Reset Standards, and Protect Authority  
Audience: Founder / CEO  

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS TOOL

Founders delay hard conversations because they fear:

- Damaging relationships
- Losing talent
- Looking harsh
- Being wrong
- Escalating conflict
- Triggering defensiveness

Delay compounds damage.

Hard conversations are:

- Standard resets
- Authority clarifications
- Performance corrections
- Boundary enforcement
- Strategic alignment moments

The Hard Conversation Protocol creates clarity without escalation.

---

# 2. WHEN TO USE

Use when:

- Performance slipping
- Behavior misaligned
- Authority unclear
- Tension building
- Expectations unmet
- Cultural drift visible
- Founder feels resentment

If resentment exists, conversation overdue.

---

# 3. PRE-CONVERSATION PREP

Before speaking, define:

## A. The Observable Facts
No emotion.
No interpretation.

Example:
“Three roadmap deadlines were missed in the last quarter.”

## B. The Standard
What should have happened?

Example:
“Deadlines committed to the executive team must be met or renegotiated early.”

## C. The Impact
What is the consequence of the gap?

Example:
“This creates unpredictability for sales and weakens confidence.”

## D. The Desired Outcome
What must change?

Example:
“Commitments must either be met or escalated before risk.”

If you cannot articulate these cleanly, you are not ready.

---

# 4. THE OPENING FRAME

Start with:

“I want to talk about something important because the standard matters.”

Direct.
Calm.
Grounded.

Avoid:

- Over-softening
- Apologizing for bringing it up
- Excessive small talk

Authority sets tone.

---

# 5. THE FACTS PHASE

State:

1. Observed behavior
2. Pattern if applicable
3. Impact

No adjectives.
No character attacks.

Example:

“In the last 6 weeks, pricing decisions were escalated 5 times after agreement.”

Pause.

Let them respond.

---

# 6. THE STANDARD RESET

State clearly:

“This is the standard going forward.”

Be specific.

Example:

“Pricing under $100K is fully owned by you. No escalation required.”

Avoid:

- “I’d prefer”
- “It would be great if”
- “Maybe we could”

Standards are not suggestions.

---

# 7. THE OWNERSHIP TRANSFER

Ask:

“What’s your understanding of the standard?”

Let them articulate.

Then ask:

“What would prevent this from happening again?”

This surfaces friction early.

---

# 8. CONSEQUENCE CLARITY

If performance-related:

State:

“If this continues, we will need to reconsider role fit.”

Calm.
Not threatening.
Clear.

Consequence without hostility.

---

# 9. CLOSE WITH ALIGNMENT

End with:

“Are we aligned on the expectation?”

Wait for explicit confirmation.

No ambiguous endings.

---

# 10. POWER POSITIONING RULES

During conversation:

- Slow pace
- Lower tone
- Hold silence
- Maintain eye contact
- Do not over-explain
- Do not debate standards
- Do not justify authority
- Avoid emotional escalation

Confidence reduces defensiveness.

---

# 11. SPECIAL CASES

## A. Underperformance

Focus on behavior and output.
Not personality.

## B. Executive Misalignment

Reset authority boundaries.

## C. Co-founder Conflict

Frame around role clarity and company interest.
Not ego.

## D. Cultural Violation

Reinforce company values.
Public example may be required later.

---

# 12. FAILURE MODES

AI must guard against:

- Over-soft language
- Turning conversation into debate
- Emotional venting
- Lack of consequence clarity
- Ambiguous standards
- Attempting to win argument
- Seeking validation

This is about clarity, not persuasion.

---

# 13. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES

If another AI helps prepare for a hard conversation:

It must:

1. Clarify observable facts.
2. Define standard explicitly.
3. Define impact.
4. Craft direct opening.
5. Include ownership question.
6. Include consequence clarity.
7. Avoid emotional language.
8. Produce script-ready structure.

Tone:

- Direct
- Calm
- Firm
- Controlled
- Respectful

Never:

- Encourage aggressive tone.
- Encourage manipulation.
- Avoid consequences.
- Over-intellectualize.
