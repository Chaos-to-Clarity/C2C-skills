# Chaos to Clarity Council

Run any plan past your department heads before you bet the quarter on it.

You bring the work: a plan, a PRD, a pricing change, a launch, a hiring plan, a fundraising deck, or a decision you've been circling. A CEO seats the departments with a real stake in it. Each seat reviews the work alone, through its own lens, rubric, and bookshelf. Then the CEO weighs every review, makes the call, and gives you a brief of the changes with the reasoning from each department. Say "implement," and you get the revised version of your work.

## The seats

| Seat | Archetype | The question it always asks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | The Problem Hunter | What problem does this solve, for whom, and how do we know they'd pay to have it solved? |
| Design | The Experience Architect | Walk me through the first five minutes from the customer's point of view. Where do they get stuck? |
| Engineering | The Systems Realist | What has to be true technically for this to work, and who on the team is going to build and run it? |
| Operations | The Load-Bearing Operator | Who owns this, what changes in how we work on Monday, and what breaks if it goes well? |
| Marketing | The Positioning Strategist | Can a buyer in our ICP repeat back what this is, why it's different, and why it matters now? |
| Sales | The Quota Carrier | Will this help a rep close a real deal this quarter, and what will the buyer object to? |
| Customer Success | The Retention Guardian | Which existing customers get hurt by this, and who churns because of it? |
| Finance | The Runway Keeper | What does this cost in cash and runway, when does it pay back, and what happens if it doesn't work? |
| Legal | The Risk Underwriter | What does this commit us to in writing, and what's the worst realistic outcome if we get it wrong? |
| CEO | The Clarity CEO | What's the one thing that, if we get it right, makes everything else easier? |

Every seat carries a weighted rubric, hard stops that force a block, guidance for Series A and Series B, its known blind spots, and a shelf of books it draws on when the work goes past its own experience. Product, Engineering, and Marketing draw on *Growth by Design*. The CEO draws on *Above the Chaos*.

## How a review runs

1. **Intake.** The CEO restates your decision and asks up to three questions, only where the answer changes the ruling.
2. **Seating.** The CEO seats three to six departments using a rubric: who owns it, where the damage lands if it fails, and how hard it is to undo. Some facts seat a department automatically, such as any pricing change seating Finance, Sales, and Customer Success. You can add or drop seats, or say "full council."
3. **Sealed reviews.** Each seat reviews alone and never sees the others, so they can't drift toward agreement.
4. **The challenger.** If the council agrees too easily, a challenger runs a premortem and argues for the alternative you rejected.
5. **The Council Brief.** The call, the changes, each department's reasoning, the dissent that was overruled, your constraint and leverage point, your next 48 hours, and one hard question.
6. **Implement.** Approve all the changes or pick by number. You get the revised work in its original format, with placeholders wherever a fact is missing and a change log at the end.

## Get started

Install the plugin (see the [main page](../../README.md)), then attach or paste your work and say **"Run the council on this."**

Runs best where Claude can launch subagents (Claude Code or the Claude desktop app), so every seat reviews in true isolation. It also works in a plain chat, where the seats review one at a time. A full council uses up to ten agents, so it costs more tokens than a single answer.

Pairs well with [Growth by Design](../growth-by-design) when the council flags a product-market fit gap, [Above the Chaos](../above-the-chaos) when the decision is really about how you lead, and [Cortex](https://github.com/chaostoclarity/Cortex) so the council can read your strategy and past decisions.
