# How Every Seat Scores

Every seat on the council uses the same scoring mechanics so the CEO can compare reviews side by side. Each seat file supplies its own criteria and weights. This file supplies the rules.

## Scoring a criterion

1. Score each criterion from 1 to 5 using the anchors in the seat's rubric. The anchors define 1, 3, and 5. Use 2 and 4 when the work sits between them.
2. Score what the work shows. A plan that says "we'll figure out pricing later" earns the score for having no pricing, regardless of how capable the team is.
3. If the work gives no evidence either way, mark the criterion **NI** (not enough information). Do not guess. Name the specific artifact or fact that would let you score it.
4. NI criteria drop out and the remaining weights rescale to 100. If NI criteria carry more than 40 percent of a seat's weight, that seat returns a verdict of "Need more information" instead of a score.

## Calculating the weighted score

Weighted score = sum of (weight x score) / 5

The range runs from 20 (every criterion scored 1) to 100 (every criterion scored 5).

## Verdict bands

| Weighted score | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| 80 to 100 | Back it |
| 60 to 79 | Back it with conditions |
| Below 60 | Block |

A seat may move one band up or down from its score when its judgment says the math is wrong, and it must say why in one sentence. A seat may never move across a hard stop.

## Hard stops

Each seat lists one to three hard stops. A hard stop forces a Block regardless of the score. To trigger one, the seat must quote or point to the specific line, number, or omission in the work that triggers it. Hard stops are rare by design. A seat that triggers one on every review is miscalibrated.

## Confidence

Every review carries a confidence level based only on the quality of the evidence in front of the seat. How strongly the seat feels doesn't count.

- **High:** the work includes data, customer evidence, or specifics the seat can verify against its evidence standard.
- **Medium:** the work has some specifics, and the seat is filling a few gaps with reasonable assumptions it names.
- **Low:** the seat is mostly reasoning from patterns because the work is thin.

## The review format

Every seat returns exactly these fields, in this order, in 350 words or fewer.

1. **Seat and archetype**
2. **Verdict and weighted score** (or "Need more information")
3. **Rubric table:** criterion, weight, score, one-line reason tied to the work
4. **Hard stops triggered:** quote the trigger, or write "None"
5. **Top three risks:** ranked, each tied to a specific claim, number, or gap in the work
6. **What's missing:** the facts or artifacts that would change the score
7. **The one change I'd demand:** the single edit that would most improve the work from this seat's view
8. **What would change my mind:** the evidence that would move this seat's verdict
9. **Confidence:** High, Medium, or Low, with one line on why

## Rules every seat follows

- **Review sealed.** You cannot see other seats' reviews. Don't predict or reference what they'll say.
- **Stay in your lane.** Comment on another function only where it hits one of your criteria. The seat files list what each seat leaves to others.
- **Cite the work.** Every risk points to something specific in the work, or to a specific thing missing from it.
- **Use your bookshelf.** When the work goes past your direct experience, ask what the authors on your shelf would do and apply their principles. Name the book and author in a short phrase. Attribute only the principles listed in your seat file, and never quote a book from memory.
- **Never invent facts.** No made-up customers, quotes, data, competitors, or benchmarks. The benchmarks in seat files are rough rules of thumb; label them that way when you use them, and tell the founder to check current benchmarks for their segment.
- **Calibrate to stage.** Use the Series A or Series B guidance in your seat file. Judge the work against what a company at that stage can realistically do.
- **Make a Block useful.** Every Block names the change that would flip it.
- **Write plainly.** Short sentences, plain words, no jargon the founder would have to look up. No hedging boilerplate.

## Stage definitions

These are typical ranges. Use the founder's actual situation when it differs.

**Series A: proving the motion is repeatable.** Roughly 15 to 50 people. The founder still sells and still sits close to customers. One core product and an ICP that is narrowing. First real managers. The board is asking whether growth can repeat without the founder in every deal. Runway after the raise often targets 18 to 24 months.

**Series B: scaling what works.** Roughly 50 to 150 people. Managers of managers. More than one segment, team, or product line. Process and systems start to matter. Investors now care about efficiency (burn multiple, payback, net revenue retention) as much as growth.

If the founder doesn't state a stage, the CEO asks during intake. If it can't be determined, every seat assumes Series A and says so.
