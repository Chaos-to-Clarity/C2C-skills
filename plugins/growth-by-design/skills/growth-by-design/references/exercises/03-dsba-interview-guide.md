## Why it matters

The most expensive product you can build is one nobody needs, and there's no shortcut for problem validation. This guide is the question bank for the five conversation stages of Don't Suck, Be Awesome (the sixth step, building it, happens after the interview). Adapt the language to the customer, but don't skip a stage.

Start by watching, not asking. Shadow people at work and take in everything about their environment. Capture every product they use. Hunt for the duct-taped solutions, where they bend a tool to a purpose it wasn't built for or wire several products together to get one job done. Those workarounds are where the best opportunities hide.

Then interview in layers, broad to specific: map the whole landscape of frustration, narrow to the one that stresses them most, go deep on it (what does it cost in time, money, and sanity?), then ask what they've tried. Listen for emotional language and write it down verbatim. The heat in their words is your severity signal, and the same words become your messaging later.

The rules: it's a conversation, not a survey. Don't ask leading questions. Don't ask people what they want; if they knew how to solve it, they'd have done it already. Don't ask yes-or-no questions. Accept the truth: if the data don't fit your beliefs, your beliefs are wrong. And never pitch. The first conversation is entirely about the problem. Concepts and "what if" scenarios come in follow-ups. Rob Fitzpatrick's The Mom Test is the book's recommended guide to interviews that don't lie to you.

## Use it when

- The founder has an interview booked and wants a script for their domain.
- Past interviews turned into demos, or produced polite praise and no new learning.
- They just finished an interview and want to capture it before details fade.
- Problem 3 or Problem 4 scored 1 or 2.

## Before you start

Ask which mode they need. **Prepare** builds a tailored script before the interview. **Debrief** walks through a completed interview stage by stage and fills the Capture fields. If they have both needs, run Prepare now and Debrief after the call.

For Prepare, you need the domain and who they're talking to. For Debrief, ask for notes or a transcript; if they're working from memory, every Capture field without a quote gets tagged [Assumption]. Anything the script assumes about the customer is tagged [Assumption] and becomes something to listen for. Never block them.

## Run it

### Mode A: Prepare

**Step 1 of 7: The person and the domain**
> Who are you talking to, and what part of their work are you exploring?

Push for: a role and a domain broad enough to surprise them. "Their reporting workflow" works; "whether they'd use our dashboard" is a pitch.

**Step 2 of 7: Shadowing plan**
> Can you watch them do this work, live or on a screen share, before you start asking?

Push for: a plan to observe every tool, and every export, copy-paste, or spreadsheet holding things together. If they can't shadow, ask for a walkthrough of a recent instance.

**Step 3 of 7: Stage 1, Know Your Customer**
> How would you open, in your own words, to learn their role, their top priority, and what success means to them this year?

Push for: adaptations of the bank: "Tell me about your role and what a typical week looks like." "What are you most responsible for hitting right now?" "What does success look like for you this year?"

**Step 4 of 7: Stage 2, Ask What Sucks**
> What's your wide, emotional question about [domain], and your follow-up that gets them to tell a recent story?

Push for: "What's the most frustrating part of [domain]?" "When does it feel hardest? Walk me through a recent moment." "What do you wish just worked without you having to think about it?" Check every question: could they answer yes or no? Does it lead them?

**Step 5 of 7: Stage 3, Find What Sucks Most**
> How will you get them to rank the pain and tell you what they've already tried?

Push for: "Of everything you mentioned, which one costs you the most time or money?" "If you could only fix one, which would it be?" "What have you tried? Why didn't it stick?" Add the cost probe: time, money, and sanity.

**Step 6 of 7: Stage 4, Picture What's Awesome**
> How will you ask what awesome looks like without asking them to design your product?

Push for: outcome questions. "Instead of [the sucky thing], what would awesome look like?" "If this were solved perfectly, what would you be able to do that you can't do now?" "What would that be worth to you?" If they draft "What features would you want?", cut it.

**Step 7 of 7: Stage 5, Sell It Back**
> What sentence will you use to repeat their problem back, and how will you confirm it?

Push for: "So it sounds like what really hurts is ___." Then: "Would it be fair to say that if ___, that would solve it?" Listen for "Exactly." That's the value prop. Remind them: this is reflection, not a pitch.

### Mode B: Debrief

Ask one question per stage, then fill the Capture field.

**Step 1 of 6: What you saw**
> What did you observe about their environment, tools, and workarounds? (Capture: tools used, duct-taped solutions)

**Step 2 of 6: Stage 1**
> What's their job title, top priority, and definition of success?

**Step 3 of 6: Stage 2**
> What problems did they mention, and what were their exact emotional words?

**Step 4 of 6: Stage 3**
> How did they rank the problems, and what workarounds have they tried?

**Step 5 of 6: Stage 4**
> In their words, what's the ideal outcome, and what signals did you hear about willingness to pay?

**Step 6 of 6: Stage 5**
> When you sold it back, what language did they confirm, and did you hear "exactly"?

Push for, throughout: quotes, not summaries. If a field is a paraphrase, ask: "What did they actually say?" Flag anything that surprised the founder; it goes in the Surprise column of #9 Customer Interview Tracker.

## Push back when

- **The script pitches.** "Never pitch during the interview. The goal is to understand, not to sell. The pitch comes later, built from what you heard."
- **A stage is missing.** "Don't skip a stage. Without Stage 3 you don't know which pain matters most."
- **They plan to ask what customers want.** "They don't know. Ask what they're trying to get done and what's in the way."
- **The debrief has no quotes.** "Paraphrase loses the heat. Without their exact words you can't judge severity or write the copy."
- **The interview contradicted their belief.** "Accept the truth. If the data don't fit your beliefs, your beliefs are wrong."

In Prepare mode, Capture fields hold what to listen for. In Debrief mode, they hold what was heard.

**Coaching notes**: 3 to 5 sentences: in Prepare, whether the script stays out of pitch mode and whether every stage is open; in Debrief, what the interview revealed, how hot the language was, and whether it confirmed or challenged their hypothesis.

**Assumptions to test**: each [Assumption] with the question to ask in the next interview.

**Your next move this week**: Prepare: book the interview and a shadowing slot. Debrief: log this interview as a row in #9 Customer Interview Tracker today.

## Done well

- Every stage has open, non-leading questions in the customer's vocabulary, and the script never mentions the product.
- The debrief holds at least three verbatim quotes, including one emotional phrase.
