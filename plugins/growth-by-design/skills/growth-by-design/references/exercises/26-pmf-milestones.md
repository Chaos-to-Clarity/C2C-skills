## Why it matters

Most founders define product-market fit the way a Supreme Court justice once defined obscenity: they'll know it when they see it. Your gut will swear you have fit long before you do, especially when you want it badly. The checklist separates "we think we have fit" from "we have evidence of fit." Marc Andreessen's version: with fit, customers buy as fast as you can serve them. Without it, every sale is a fight.

Chapter 1 names three signals. The cleanest comes from Sean Ellis, who ran growth at Dropbox and LogMeIn: ask users how they'd feel if they could no longer use the product (very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, not disappointed). When at least 40 percent say very disappointed, you're usually in PMF territory. Below it, the very disappointed group tells you which features and segment to build around. The second signal is retention. Group users by signup month and track how many are still active at one, three, and six months. If every cohort slides toward zero, you have a leaky bucket no marketing can fill. If the line flattens and holds, real fans have built you into their routine. Retention is impossible to fake. The third is virality, or organic lift: users bring in users, and inbound arrives from segments you never targeted. ChatGPT is the book's epic-scale example, shipped with no launch campaign and no sales team.

Most founders treat the 40 percent test as a one-time grade. Rahul Vohra at Superhuman turned it into a habit: he surveyed constantly, studied who would be very disappointed and what they loved, and pointed the roadmap at creating more of them. NPS, active users, and growth rates are supporting evidence. They tell you whether you have fit, not why.

## Use it when

- The founder says "I think we have product-market fit" or is about to raise on that claim.
- Before scaling spend or headcount.
- Quarterly, as a re-check against the last run.

## Before you start

Set the bar: a box gets checked only if they can point to the evidence. Ask them to have their interview notes, survey results, retention or churn data, channel numbers, and unit economics handy. If something's missing, mark it unchecked and tag it [Assumption], and make collecting it the next move. Don't block them.

If they ran this before, ask for the last result so you can compare.

## Run it

Go section by section. Ask each check as its own question, one at a time, and demand the evidence before checking it.

**Step 1 of 4: Early Signal (Pre-PMF)**
> Let's start with early signal. Can you honestly confirm each of these, and show me what proves it?

Ask in turn, with the evidence to demand:
1. 15+ customer discovery interviews in the last 90 days. *The count and dates, from #9 if they use it.*
2. You can name the specific problem and who it hurts most. *One sentence, then: "Which customers said that?"*
3. At least one customer said they'd be "very disappointed" without you. *Who, when, and their words.*
4. At least one customer found you without you pushing them toward it. *Their name and how they found you.*
5. You've shipped an MVP and someone used it to completion without hand-holding. *Who, which task, and whether you watched or logged it.*

**Step 2 of 4: Approaching Fit**
> Now approaching fit. Same rule: evidence first.

1. 40%+ of surveyed users say "very disappointed" (Sean Ellis test). *Survey date, how many responded, the percent. If they haven't run it, offer to design one now (see below).*
2. You can name the one channel where customers find you predictably. *The channel and its volume over time, from #22 if done.*
3. NPS or CSAT is positive and trending up. *At least two readings, with dates.*
4. LTV:CAC is above 3:1, or there's a credible path within 12 months. *The actual LTV and CAC, from #23. "Credible" means the inputs are measured.*
5. At least three unsolicited referrals from existing customers. *Three names.*

**Sean Ellis survey offer.** If they haven't run one, offer to help design it: the single question, the three answer options, who to send it to (real users), and how to read it. Have them tag each response with who answered, so they can study the very disappointed group the way Superhuman did and build the roadmap around creating more of them.

**Step 3 of 4: Confirmed Fit**
> Last section, confirmed fit.

1. Revenue is growing at a rate you can sustain without heroic effort. *The growth rate, and: "Would it keep growing if you stopped selling personally?"*
2. Churn is below industry average, or net revenue retention is above 100%. *The number and the benchmark it beats. Ask whether the retention cohorts flatten or slide toward zero.*
3. You've won customers from the alternatives you set out to beat. *Named wins and why they switched.*
4. Your ICP is proven: tight enough to disqualify a bad fit in one call. *The ICP and a recent lead it ruled out, from #7.*
5. The team can state your PMF story in one sentence without disagreeing. *Ask two team members separately and compare.*

**Step 4 of 4: Read the result**
> Of the boxes you couldn't check, which one would change the business most if you could check it in the next 90 days?

Push for: one gap, chosen against the lowest pillar on #1 PMF Scorecard. If they've checked later-tier boxes with earlier-tier gaps, name the mismatch.

## Push back when

- **A box is checked on belief.** "What can you point to? If it's a feeling, it stays unchecked."
- **Friendly anecdotes stand in for the survey.** "One happy customer isn't 40 percent. When did you last ask everyone?"
- **Growth is all push.** "If it stops when you stop pushing, the boulder is still going uphill."
- **Retention is skipped.** "Retention can't be faked. Do your cohorts flatten or slide toward zero?"
- **All fifteen are checked.** The workbook's rule: checking all fifteen doesn't mean you're done. PMF erodes. Re-check every quarter.

**Coaching notes**: 3 to 5 sentences on what the checklist revealed, for example which tier they're really in, where belief stood in for evidence, and what retention says.

**Assumptions to test**: each unchecked or [Assumption] box with the cheapest way to get the evidence (often the Sean Ellis survey or a cohort retention pull).

**Your next move this week**: one concrete action, usually "send the Sean Ellis survey to every active user" or "pull retention by signup month."

## Done well

- Every checked box has evidence the founder named out loud.
- The founder knows which tier they're actually in.
- The Sean Ellis test has been run, or is scheduled with a design.
- A re-check date is on the calendar.
