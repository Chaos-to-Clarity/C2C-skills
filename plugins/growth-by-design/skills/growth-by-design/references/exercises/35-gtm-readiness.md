## Why it matters

This is the gate before scaling spend or headcount. The book opens with the pattern behind most failures Eric has seen: "They built before they understood, and they scaled before they had fit." The operating system exists to defer the expensive work of scaling until the problem is understood and the solution validated.

The checklist pulls four foundations from earlier chapters. **Product ready** comes from Chapter 6: the Aha Moment is when a customer goes from "I'm listening" to "I get it," and onboarding exists to get them there fast, measured with NPS or CSAT at key touchpoints. **Message ready** comes from Chapter 9: a positioning statement in April Dunford's terms (Obviously Awesome), and the simplest test there is, reading copy to a real customer and having them say it back. **Channel ready** comes from Chapter 10: find the one channel that works, with a target CAC, conversion, and payback, the way the Bullseye Framework in Traction (Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares) narrows the field. **Model ready** comes from Chapter 11: David Skok's benchmarks of LTV to CAC around three to one and payback under roughly twelve months, with gross margin underneath. A weak model can look exactly like weak fit.

Chapter 19 adds the frame: product-market fit is something you keep re-earning as you grow into new segments and stages. Passing once doesn't mean it stays passed. The book's model of winning before widening is Superhuman, which onboarded high-volume email users one by one until they were hooked, then expanded from strength.

## Use it when

- The founder is about to raise, hire a sales team, or turn up paid acquisition.
- Growth has stalled and the reflex is to spend more.
- After #26 PMF Milestones Checklist shows approaching or confirmed fit.
- Entering a new segment or stage, since fit has to be re-earned.

## Before you start

Ask what scaling decision is on the table and when. Ask them to have numbers at hand: onboarding completion, NPS or CSAT, churn reasons, CAC by channel, funnel conversion, LTV, gross margin.

Every check needs evidence: a number, a document, a customer quote. A box checked on belief stays unchecked and gets tagged [Assumption]. Don't block the founder for missing data; an unchecked box routes them to the exercise that fixes it.

## Run it

Go section by section. For each item, ask the question, demand the evidence, then mark it checked, unchecked, or [Assumption].

**Step 1 of 5: Product ready**
> Let's start with product. Can new users complete the core task without help, and how do you know?

Then, one at a time: What's your onboarding completion rate, and what's your threshold? (Set the number for the blank.) Does the Aha Moment happen in the first session for most users? Is NPS or CSAT positive, and can you name what's driving it? Do you understand the root cause of churn, and is a fix in progress? Push for: completion data, session recordings, the actual score and its drivers, churn reasons from real customers. Unchecked routes to **#14 Experience Canvas**; churn with no known cause also points to #31 Customer Feedback Log.

**Step 2 of 5: Message ready**
> Has a real customer heard your one-liner and repeated it back the way you meant it?

Then: Is the positioning statement documented, and does the whole team use it? Do you have at least three customer proof points (quotes, case studies, data)? Do the website and sales materials use customer language, not internal jargon? Push for: the customer's restatement, the document itself, the three proof points named. Unchecked routes to **#20 Messaging Canvas**.

**Step 3 of 5: Channel ready**
> Which channel delivers leads at a CAC you can sustain, and what's the number?

Then: Do you know conversion at each funnel stage? Is the ICP defined tightly enough to disqualify a bad fit in one conversation? Are the repeatable steps to close a deal documented? Push for: CAC by channel, stage-by-stage conversion, the written ICP, the playbook. Channel gaps route to **#21 Channel Canvas** and **#22 Channel Testing Log**; an ICP gap routes to **#7 Persona Canvas**.

**Step 4 of 5: Model ready**
> What's your LTV to CAC today, and if it's under 3:1, what's the plan to get there within 12 months?

Then: Does gross margin support the growth model you're planning? Has pricing been tested with real customers, not just modeled? Are unit economics documented and shared with the team? Push for: the actual numbers and the test results. Unchecked routes to **#23 Model Canvas** and **#24 Pricing Test Canvas**.

**Step 5 of 5: The verdict**
> With these gaps in front of you, is scaling the right move now, or which box do you fix first?

Count the checks per section. All checked: clear to scale, with a date to re-check. Otherwise, name the weakest section, start with the most upstream gap, and pick one exercise to run this week.

## Push back when

- **Boxes get checked from memory.** "What's the number? If you have to guess, it's unchecked."
- **The plan is to scale and fix later.** "If you can't check every box, scaling will amplify the gap, not close it."
- **CAC is blended across channels.** "Which channel? A blended number hides the one that's losing money."
- **LTV:CAC rests on projected LTV.** "Pricing tested with real customers, or modeled? Modeled doesn't count."
- **They passed last year.** "Fit gets re-earned as you grow into new segments and stages. When did you last check?"

**Coaching notes**: 3 to 5 sentences on readiness, for example which section is weakest, whether gaps sit upstream, and whether the scaling decision should wait.

**Assumptions to test**: each [Assumption] check, with the fastest way to get the evidence (a data pull, five rephrase tests, a pricing test).

**Your next move this week**: one action, usually "run the exercise for the most upstream unchecked box before committing any new spend."

## Done well

- Every checked box has a number, document, or customer quote behind it.
- Every unchecked box has a named exercise and an owner.
- The founder makes the scaling call based on the count, not the pressure.
