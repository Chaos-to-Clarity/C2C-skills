## Why it matters

Persona is the first pillar because every other pillar rests on it. You can't define a problem worth solving, set a price, or pick a channel until you know exactly whose problem it is. The book's core line: you are not your customer. Even founders who used to be the customer drift away from that lived experience the moment they start building, and every year it gets easier to design for yourself and assume the market thinks like you do. Lose that understanding and you spend years tuning the other nine pillars for a customer who only exists in your head.

The fix starts with going narrow. Most founders aim at "small businesses" or "developers" and resonate with no one. Pick a beachhead: one small, homogeneous group you can win completely before you expand. Geoffrey Moore (Crossing the Chasm) and Bill Aulet (Disciplined Entrepreneurship) both build on this. Choose it by four hard criteria: the pain is urgent, the people are reachable, they have money to spend, and they talk to each other. Superhuman is the book's example: Rahul Vohra didn't open the app to everyone who wanted faster email. He started with high-volume professionals who lived in their inbox and onboarded each one personally until they were hooked.

A persona is only useful if it's built from primary research. Research and survey the market, cluster people into segments, then interview one real individual per segment to fill in the psychographics a demographic profile never shows. To keep honest feedback flowing, the founder needs three things with this group: access (easy, regular contact with many of them), empathy (setting aside their own assumptions), and trust (so people tell the truth and tolerate a messy process). If customers won't even talk to you, you'll have a much harder time selling to them.

## Use it when

- The founder describes their customer as a broad category ("SMBs", "teams", "anyone who...").
- Sales calls keep landing with bad-fit leads, or the team disagrees about who the customer is.
- Persona scored under 3, or Persona 5 (disqualify a bad fit in under a minute) scored 1 or 2.
- Before Messaging or Channel work, since both depend on knowing exactly who you're talking to.

## Before you start

Ask how many target customers they've talked to in the last 90 days and whether they have notes. The canvas is meant to be filled from real research. If they have fewer than five conversations, say so plainly: most fields will be guesses. Offer two paths: run #3 DSBA Interview Guide and #9 Customer Interview Tracker first, or build a proto-persona now (the book's quick version) with every unsupported field tagged [Assumption], so the canvas becomes their interview plan. Don't block them either way.

If they serve several segments, make them pick one for this canvas. One canvas, one beachhead.

## Run it

**Step 1 of 9: Segment**
> Who is the one narrow group you're going to win first? Describe them tightly enough that you could find 50 of them by name.

Push for: a group defined by role, situation, and context, not a category. If they say "SMBs", ask: "Which kind, doing what, feeling which pain right now?" Check it against the four beachhead criteria (urgent pain, reachable, money to spend, talk to each other) and name any that fail.

**Step 2 of 9: Ideal Customer Profile**
> What traits make someone a fit, and what traits instantly disqualify them?

Push for: both lists. The disqualifiers matter most. Test it: "If a lead called you right now, could you rule them out in under a minute with this?" If not, keep tightening.

**Step 3 of 9: Wants**
> What do these people aspire to? What would make them proud at work or in their life?

Push for: aspirations in the customer's words, not features. Ask: "Who told you that, and what did they actually say?"

**Step 4 of 9: Needs**
> What do they require to get their job done? What's non-negotiable for them?

Push for: concrete requirements (time, budget, approvals, tools they must work with). Separate needs from wants.

**Step 5 of 9: Frustrations**
> What's in their way today? What do they complain about?

Push for: emotional language, verbatim if possible. "I freaking hate it when..." beats "it's kind of annoying." Capture quotes; they feed #20 Messaging Canvas.

**Step 6 of 9: Fears**
> What risk or outcome are they trying to avoid? What keeps them up at night?

Push for: the career, status, or financial risk underneath the frustration. Fears often reveal the real buying trigger.

**Step 7 of 9: Where they gather, and who they trust**
> Where do these people hang out, online and off, and whose recommendations do they actually act on?

Push for: named places (a specific subreddit, Slack community, conference, newsletter, analyst, creator). "LinkedIn" alone isn't an answer. This field feeds #21 Channel Canvas directly.

**Step 8 of 9: SOM, bottom-up**
> How many of these people can you realistically reach, and what would each one spend per year? Multiply them.

Push for: a count from real sources, not a percentage of a market report. Skip the "fifty billion dollar market" math. If they haven't sized it, offer #10 Beachhead Market Sizing next.

**Step 9 of 9: Buying roles (B2B only)**
> Who uses it, who pays for it, and who champions it internally? Are those the same person?

Push for: three named roles. When they're different people, ask which one the founder has actually talked to. Deals that stall after enthusiastic pilots usually come from talking only to the user. Skip this step for B2C.

## Push back when

- **The segment is a category.** "That's a market, not a beachhead. Who inside it feels the pain most urgently and talks to each other?"
- **Every field sounds like the founder.** "Is this them, or you? You are not your customer. Which of these came from a conversation?"
- **The ICP disqualifies no one.** "If nobody fails this test, it isn't a test. What would make you walk away from a deal?"
- **Fields are filled but nothing is sourced.** Tag them [Assumption] without argument, and turn each into an interview question. The workbook's rule: if you can't fill a field from real research, that's your next interview goal.
- **They resist going narrow.** "Narrow is how you win first. You expand from strength, once this group is yours."

**Coaching notes**: 3 to 5 sentences on what the canvas revealed, for example how much is assumption, whether the beachhead passes the four criteria, and whether the ICP can actually disqualify a lead.

**Assumptions to test**: each [Assumption] field with the interview question that would settle it.

**Your next move this week**: one concrete action, usually "book five interviews with people who match this segment" with the first three names.

## Done well

- A stranger could find ten of these people from the Segment and ICP alone.
- The ICP rules out a bad-fit lead in under a minute.
- Frustrations and Fears are in the customer's words, with quotes.
- Fewer than half the fields are tagged [Assumption], or the founder has a dated plan to fix that.
