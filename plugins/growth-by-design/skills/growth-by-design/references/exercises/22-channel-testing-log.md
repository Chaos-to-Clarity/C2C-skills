## Why it matters

You can't chase every channel at once. The Bullseye Framework from Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares's Traction says to brainstorm widely, test the three most promising cheaply, then pour your effort into the single one that gives the strongest signal. This log is where that discipline lives. Run one channel at a time and track results against the kill-or-scale threshold you set before you started, then move to the next.

Every test is an experiment: a target customer acquisition cost, a measured conversion rate, and a payback period, so you know which channel actually pays for itself. Channels keep evolving (AI-assisted content and SEO, niche communities and marketplaces, creator partnerships) and they're cheaper to test than ever. The discipline stays the same: test small, kill what doesn't work, double down on what does.

The mistake founders make is never deciding. A channel that's "kind of working" eats a quarter of budget and attention while nothing gets killed and nothing gets scaled. The other mistake is forgetting what was ruled out, then retrying it six months later with no new reason. The log fixes both: a verdict on every row and a record of what you learned. The prize is Channel 5, one channel that reaches customers predictably and repeatably, with a known CAC (Channel 4).

## Use it when

- The founder is running several channels and can't say which one works.
- Channel 4 (CAC by channel) or Channel 5 (one repeatable channel) scored 1 or 2.
- A test from #21 Channel Canvas is starting or just finished.
- Spend is about to scale and nobody has checked CAC against payback.

## Before you start

Ideally #21 Channel Canvas is done, with three channels and a target CAC, conversion goal, and kill-or-scale threshold for each. If not, set the target and threshold for each channel in this session before logging it. A row with no threshold set in advance can't produce an honest verdict.

Ask how they measure acquisition: tracked signups by source, UTM links, a CRM field, or asking new customers how they heard. If attribution is missing, actual CAC is an [Assumption]; the first fix is tracking conversion and signups by source.

This log grows over time. If the founder has a prior log, ask them to paste it and add new rows rather than starting fresh. Loop row by row, then do the summary fields.

## Run it

**Step 1 of 4: Pick the row**
> Which one channel are you logging now, and is the test planned, running, or done?

Push for: one channel. If they're running several at once, log each separately and flag that overlapping tests muddy attribution.

**Step 2 of 4: Fill the row** (repeat per channel)

Ask one at a time, acknowledging each answer:

> Which motion does this channel serve: PLG, sales, community, founder-led, or discovery-led?

> What target CAC did you set before you started?

> What's the actual CAC so far: total spend, including time, divided by customers won?

> What's the conversion rate, from what step to what step?

> How much budget and time have you put in?

> Against your threshold, is this kill, scale, or keep testing until a set date?

Push for: real numbers with a source. Actual CAC must count founder time if that's the main cost. Conversion needs a defined start and end (visit to signup, demo to close). For the verdict, compare actual to target and threshold. "Keep testing" needs an end date and what would change the answer.

**Step 3 of 4: Proven channel and next test**
> Which one channel would you bet the quarter on right now?

Then:
> What's the next channel to test, and why that one?

Push for: a proven channel only if it hit the threshold and repeated. If none qualifies, the answer is "not yet." The next test should come from the three in #21, or from where customers gather.

**Step 4 of 4: Ruled out**
> Which channels have you ruled out, and what exactly did you learn from each?

Push for: a reason tied to data (CAC too high, wrong audience, conversion below threshold) and whether the cause was the channel, the message, or the timing. A bad message can kill a good channel; note it if so.

## Push back when

- **No threshold was set in advance.** "Without a kill-or-scale number decided up front, you'll talk yourself into keeping it. What's the number?"
- **Several channels running at once.** "Run one at a time, or you won't know what caused what."
- **Actual CAC ignores founder time.** "Your hours cost something. What's the CAC if you count them?"
- **A channel is "kind of working" for months.** "Kill it or scale it. Give it a date and a number."
- **The verdict is made on a handful of results.** "Is that enough to decide? What sample would you trust?"
- **The workbook's rule:** "Until you can measure a channel by its CAC and payback, you're just guessing."

## Output

```
GROWTH BY DESIGN · Channel Testing Log
[Company] · [Date]

| Channel | Motion (PLG / sales / community / founder-led / discovery-led) | Target CAC | Actual CAC | Conversion rate | Budget / time invested | Kill or scale? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [channel] | [motion] | [$] | [$] [E/A] | [% from __ to __] [E/A] | [$ / weeks] | [kill / scale / test until date] |

Your one proven, repeatable channel (the one you'd bet the quarter on): [channel + CAC, or "not yet"]  [Evidence/Assumption]
Your next channel to test: [channel + why + threshold]
Channels you've ruled out and why: [channel: reason]  [Evidence/Assumption]
```

[E/A] means tag the cell [Evidence] or [Assumption]. When adding to an earlier log, keep prior rows and append new ones with dates.

**Coaching notes**: 3 to 5 sentences on what the log revealed, for example whether any channel hit its threshold, whether CAC can pay back against price, and whether a ruled-out channel failed because of the message.

**Assumptions to test**: each [Assumption] cell with the fix (usually tracking signups by source, or rerunning with a defined conversion step).

**Your next move this week**: one concrete action, usually "make the kill-or-scale call on [channel] by Friday" or "start [next channel] with a fixed budget."

## Done well

- Every row has a target set before the test and a verdict after it.
- Actual CAC includes real costs, founder time included.
- Only one channel is in active testing at a time.
- Ruled-out channels have a reason that would stop a retry without new information.
