## Why it matters

Plenty of good products die because their founders never solved distribution. Peter Thiel makes the point in Zero to One: founders obsess over the product and treat distribution as an afterthought. It deserves the same design effort.

The greatest product will never sell if you don't meet customers at the right place, time, and context. Figure out three things: where they shop and gather information (a subreddit, a trade show, a Slack community, a review site), who they trust (peers, analysts, creators), and the observable trigger that flips them from suffering to shopping. Right place and right message at the wrong moment, and they'll file you away and forget.

Match the motion to the buyer and price point. Product-led growth (Wes Bush, Product-Led Growth) sells through a free trial or tier and fits low-friction tools. Sales-led puts a human in the loop for higher-priced, complex products. Community-led fits customers who already cluster in active communities. Founder-led sales is a painful but necessary step: Geoffrey Moore's work on early markets says you must sell it yourself first to learn why customers say yes or no. It's as much research as revenue. The workbook also lists discovery-led, the customer-development discipline from Steve Blank's The Four Steps to the Epiphany.

Then find the one channel that works. Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares lay out the Bullseye Framework in Traction across nineteen traction channels: brainstorm every plausible channel, pick the three most promising to test cheaply, then pour effort into the one with the strongest signal. The best channel is your own happy customers. Calendly wired distribution into the product: every link someone sends shows the recipient the product working, and many sign up to send their own.

## Use it when

- The founder says "we'll do content and some ads" with no numbers.
- Channel scored under 3, or Channel 4 (CAC by channel) or 5 (one repeatable channel) is a 1 or 2.
- Growth comes only from the founder's network and they don't know what's next.
- Before spending on acquisition.

## Before you start

Pull "Where they gather, and who they trust" from #7 Persona Canvas, and the one-liner from #20 Messaging Canvas. If the price point is known (#23 Model Canvas), use it to sanity check the motion. If the gathering places are guesses, tag them [Assumption] and add "Where do you go when you're looking for a solution like this?" to the next interviews.

## Run it

**Step 1 of 7: Where they gather, and who they trust**
> Where do your customers go to shop and gather information, and whose recommendation do they act on?

Push for: named places and named people. "LinkedIn" or "online" isn't an answer. Probe: "Which customer told you that?"

**Step 2 of 7: The buying trigger**
> What observable event flips your customer from suffering to shopping?

Push for: something visible from the outside, like the book's examples: expecting parents start searching for baby gear, a family moves into a new house, a new company is registered. Probe: "How would you know, today, that someone just hit that moment?"

**Step 3 of 7: Go-to-market motion**
> Given your price and your buyer, which motion fits: product-led, sales-led, community-led, founder-led, or discovery-led?

Push for: a motion that matches price and complexity. Name any mismatch (a cheap self-serve tool with a sales team, a complex contract with no human). Founders who want to skip founder-led sales should hear Moore's point: you learn the playbook by doing it yourself first.

**Step 4 of 7: Brainstorm, then pick three**
> List every channel that could plausibly reach these people. Which three are most promising to test cheaply right now?

Push for: a wide list first. If they stall, prompt with the nineteen channels from Traction itself (the book names the framework but doesn't list them): viral marketing, publicity, unconventional PR, search engine marketing, social and display ads, offline ads, search engine optimization, content marketing, email marketing, engineering as marketing, targeting blogs, business development, sales, affiliate programs, existing platforms, trade shows, offline events, speaking engagements, community building. Add the newer ones the book names: AI-assisted content and SEO, niche communities and marketplaces, creator partnerships. Then pick three, each tied to where they gather and the trigger.

**Step 5 of 7: Test thresholds**
> For each of the three, what's your target CAC, your conversion goal, and the number that tells you to kill it or scale it?

Push for: numbers set before the test, with a budget and time box. Check target CAC against price and payback.

**Step 6 of 7: Built-in distribution**
> How could the product spread itself every time someone uses it?

Push for: a mechanism inside the core use, like Calendly's link, plus how happy customers refer others today.

**Step 7 of 7: The repeatable channel**
> Do you have one channel today that reaches your customers predictably and repeatably? Which one, and what's its CAC?

Push for: a channel with a known CAC. If none, the answer is "not yet."

## Push back when

- **Channels chosen because competitors use them.** "Is that where your customers are, or where your competitors are?"
- **No numbers on the tests.** "Until you can measure a channel by its CAC and payback, you're just guessing."
- **Testing six channels at once.** "Pick three, test cheaply, then pour into the one that works."
- **The trigger is a feeling.** "What could you see from the outside that tells you they're ready now?"
- **Hiring sales before the founder has sold.** "Founder-led sales is research. Hand it off once you can teach the playbook."

**Coaching notes**: 3 to 5 sentences on what the canvas revealed: whether channels follow the persona's gathering places, whether the motion fits the price, whether any channel has a known CAC.

**Assumptions to test**: each [Assumption] field with the cheapest test (a small, time-boxed channel test or one interview question).

**Your next move this week**: one concrete action, usually "launch test 1 with a fixed budget and log it in #22."

## Done well

- Gathering places and trusted voices are named and sourced.
- The buying trigger is observable.
- Every test has a target CAC, conversion goal, and kill-or-scale rule set in advance.
- The motion fits the price point and buyer.
