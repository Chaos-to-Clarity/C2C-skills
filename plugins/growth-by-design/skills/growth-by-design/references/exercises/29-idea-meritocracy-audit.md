## Why it matters

The book rests Agile Product Management on five principles. Together they keep the process honest, so the product gets built for the customer on evidence instead of opinion.

**Customer-Centered.** Understand and build empathy for customers, build solutions with them rather than for them, involve them at every step, and make sure their voice is the loudest in the room. **Data-Driven.** Get personal opinions out of the room. Lean on your own biases and you build a product for an audience of one. Whenever there's a strong disagreement about what to build, the question becomes "how do we test it?" **Tightly Integrated.** Product, engineering, and customers work in one flow with no silos. The product and design team evangelizes the customer's perspective so engineers can make intuitive calls. **Least Cost to Validate.** For any idea, ask how fast and cheap you can put something in front of customers. Cut scope and polish, and invest more only once you know it meets the need. **Idea Meritocracy.** Capture and process every idea the same way, whatever its source. The CEO's brainstorm runs the same gauntlet as a request from support.

The book's example is Booking.com, which runs thousands of experiments at once and lets the results decide what ships, no matter whose idea it was. Anyone can launch a test, and the data settles the argument. Opinions start the conversation; evidence ends it. Ray Dalio (Principles) describes what an idea meritocracy takes: people put their honest thoughts on the table, they disagree thoughtfully with open minds, and they have a way to get past disagreements that remain.

The tool doesn't matter (Aha!, Productboard, ClickUp, or AI-assisted clustering of feedback). The process does: a recurring session that weighs ideas against business impact the same way every time. Hold one at least quarterly, ideally monthly.

## Use it when

- #28 Idea-Source Audit showed HIPPO or internal ideas dominating.
- Roadmap arguments get settled by seniority or volume.
- Engineers learn about customers secondhand, if at all.
- Big ideas get fully built before anyone tests them.
- There's no recurring roadmap review, or it only adds items and never removes them.

## Before you start

The best input is a finished #28 Idea-Source Audit, since it gives hard data for the Customer-Centered and Idea Meritocracy scores. If they haven't done it, proceed and tag those scores [Assumption]; suggest #28 afterward.

Ask how often they hold a roadmap planning session. If the answer is "never" or "when something breaks," note it now. It will come up under Idea Meritocracy.

Scores run 1 to 5, using the PMF Scorecard scale: 1 means no real evidence, 5 means strong, consistent evidence they can point to. A score only counts with an example behind it. If they can't name one, tag it [Assumption].

## Run it

For each principle, ask for the score with an example, then ask for the one change. Steps 1 to 5 each have two parts; ask them one at a time.

**Step 1 of 6: Customer-Centered**
> Is the customer's voice the loudest in the room when you decide what to build? Score it 1 to 5 and give me the last decision that proves it.

Then: "What's one change that would raise it?" Push for: a specific recent decision. If the founder can't recall a customer quote that changed a call, the score is 2 or lower.

**Step 2 of 6: Data-Driven**
> When your team disagrees about what to build, do you ask "how do we test it?" Score it and tell me about the last disagreement.

Then the one change. Push for: how the last argument actually ended. If it ended with "the founder decided," say so.

**Step 3 of 6: Tightly Integrated**
> Are there silos between product, engineering, and customers? Score it. When did an engineer last talk to a customer?

Then the one change. Push for: a date. "They see the tickets" doesn't count as contact.

**Step 4 of 6: Least Cost to Validate**
> What's the cheapest test for your next big idea? Score how often you test before you build.

Then the one change. Push for: a concrete test for an idea currently on the roadmap. Probe: "What could you put in front of five customers this week instead of building it?"

**Step 5 of 6: Idea Meritocracy**
> Does every idea face the same scrutiny, whatever its source, including yours? Score it.

Then the one change. Push for: whether a founder idea has ever been killed by evidence. If not, the score is low.

**Step 6 of 6: Fix the lowest first**
> Which principle scored lowest, and what's the first step on that change this week?

The workbook's rule: pick the lowest-scoring principle and fix that one first. If two tie, pick the one the founder controls most directly. Confirm there's a recurring roadmap review on the calendar, at least quarterly.

## Push back when

- **Scores are high and examples are vague.** "Give me the last time that happened. If you can't, it's a 2."
- **The founder exempts their own ideas.** "The CEO's idea should face the same gauntlet as a support ticket. When did one of yours get tested and lose?"
- **"How do we test it?" gets answered with "we'll build it and see."** "That's the most expensive test available. What's the least cost to validate?"
- **The changes are aspirations.** "'Be more customer-centered' isn't a change. What will you do differently in the next planning meeting?"
- **They want to fix all five.** "Fix the lowest one. The rest can wait a cycle."

**Coaching notes**: 3 to 5 sentences on the pattern, for example whether the founder is the HIPPO, whether disagreements get tested or decided, and how far engineers sit from customers.

**Assumptions to test**: each [Assumption] score, with the evidence that would confirm it (a meeting to observe, a data pull, a question to the team).

**Your next move this week**: one action tied to the lowest principle, for example "at the next disagreement, stop and write down how you'd test it before anyone decides."

## Done well

- Every score has a real, recent example attached.
- Each change is a behavior someone could observe next week.
- The founder applies the same scrutiny to their own ideas.
- A recurring roadmap review is on the calendar.
