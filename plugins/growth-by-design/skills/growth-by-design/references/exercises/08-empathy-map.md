## Why it matters

You are not your customer. The moment you start building, you become the one person on earth who understands your product completely, and you can never see it from their perspective again. The empathy map is a deliberate way back into their head. It captures a snapshot of your user's mind at a single moment: what they think, feel, see, hear, say, and do.

Where the persona captures who someone is in general, the empathy map captures what they're experiencing right now. That's why you fill it more than once: first for the moment they're stuck in the problem, then again while they're using your solution. The gap between the two maps is the change your product makes in their life. Later, as you build journey maps and user flows, create an empathy map at each stage to see what the user is thinking and feeling as they move through the product.

The quadrants pull against each other on purpose. What people say and what they do often differ, and Chapter 15 makes the same point: watching people use a product reveals their actual behavior, which often differs from what they tell you. Thinks & Feels holds what they might never say out loud. Pains and Gains distill the rest, and they line up with the pains and gains in Osterwalder's Value Proposition Canvas that Chapter 9 uses for messaging.

You gather this the way the book says to gather the whole User Perspective: surveying, shadowing, immersing yourself in their world, getting close enough to call them friends. The founder's mistake is filling the map from imagination. A map built at a desk describes the founder. The book closes the chapter with Steve Jobs: "Get closer than ever to your customers. So close, in fact, that you tell them what they need well before they realize it themselves."

## Use it when

- The founder has a persona but the team still can't feel what the customer feels.
- Interviews produced problems but no sense of the emotional moment.
- They're designing onboarding or a key flow and need the user's state of mind at that step.
- Persona 2 (we personally know, empathize with, and care about these people) scored under 3.

## Before you start

Ask which moment this map is for. Default to the stuck-in-the-problem moment. If they have a product in use, offer a second map for the moment they're using it. For a journey stage, name the stage first (#14 Experience Canvas covers the journey).

Anchor it to one real person from an interview or shadowing session, ideally someone from #7 Persona Canvas's segment. If they've observed nobody, build the map anyway with every quadrant tagged [Assumption]; it becomes a shadowing checklist. Never block them.

## Run it

**Step 1 of 7: The moment**
> Which person, and which exact moment? Describe the scene: where they are, what they're trying to do, what just went wrong.

Push for: one specific moment, ideally one the founder saw or heard about in detail. "During month-end close, when the numbers don't match" beats "when they do accounting."

**Step 2 of 7: Thinks & Feels**
> In that moment, what's going through their head, and how do they feel?

Push for: the private thoughts and emotions, including ones they wouldn't say to a boss. Probe: "What are they worried this says about them?"

**Step 3 of 7: Sees**
> What's in front of them? What do they see around them, on screen and off?

Push for: concrete things: tools, screens, piles of work, what colleagues or competitors are doing. Shadowing answers this best.

**Step 4 of 7: Says & Does**
> What do they say out loud in that moment, and what do they actually do?

Push for: quotes and observed actions, kept separate. Ask: "Is there a gap between what they say and what they do?" That gap is often the real insight.

**Step 5 of 7: Hears**
> What are they hearing from their boss, their peers, their customers, the people they trust?

Push for: specific voices and messages. This shows who influences them and feeds #21 Channel Canvas later.

**Step 6 of 7: Pains**
> Pulling it together, what hurts most in this moment: frustrations, obstacles, risks?

Push for: the pains in their words, with the emotional heat intact. These feed #11 Problem Canvas.

**Step 7 of 7: Gains**
> What would a win look like for them here? What would make this moment feel good?

Push for: the outcome they want, in their terms. If the founder describes their product, ask: "What would they feel, not what would they use?" These feed #20 Messaging Canvas.

If they're doing a second map (using the solution), repeat Steps 1 to 7 for that moment, then compare the two side by side.

## Push back when

- **Every quadrant sounds like the founder.** "You are not your customer. Which of these did you see or hear from a real person?"
- **Says & Does blends together.** "Split them. What they say and what they do often differ, and the difference matters."
- **The moment is generic.** "That's a role, not a moment. When exactly were they stuck?"
- **Gains are features.** "That's your solution. What do they want to feel?"
- **Only one map, for the solution moment.** "Start where they're stuck. Without the before, you can't show the change."

**Coaching notes**: 3 to 5 sentences: how much came from observation versus imagination, the most revealing gap between says and does, and what the pains and gains suggest for the product and messaging.

**Assumptions to test**: each [Assumption] quadrant with how to fill it, usually a shadowing session or a specific interview question.

**Your next move this week**: shadow one customer through the moment you mapped, and correct the map from what you see.

## Done well

- The map describes one person in one specific moment.
- Says & Does includes at least one verbatim quote and one observed action.
- Pains and Gains are in the customer's language.
- Thinks & Feels surfaces something the founder hadn't considered before.
