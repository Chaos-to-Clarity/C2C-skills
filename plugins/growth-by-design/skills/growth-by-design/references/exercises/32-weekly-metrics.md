## Why it matters

Under the per-feature metrics sits one number that captures the core value customers get. Amplitude coined it as the North Star Metric. The book's examples: Slack found that once a team exchanged about 2,000 messages it almost never left, so that became the goal for every new team. Medium measures Total Time Reading instead of pageviews, rewarding attention over clicks. Facebook's early growth team fixated on getting a new user to seven friends in ten days, the point where it became a habit. Each rises only when customers are genuinely getting value.

A real North Star measures delivered value, not activity. Signups, downloads, and pageviews climb when marketing works, even if the product fails everyone who tries it. Pick the metric that can't rise unless customers win, then put a handful of input metrics beneath it: the things the team can actually move. Eric Ries (The Lean Startup) gives the test for a good outcome metric: actionable (tied to a decision), accessible (the team can see and understand it), and auditable (it reflects real behavior you can trust). Total registered users fails all three; weekly active customers who completed the core job passes. Split leading measures (how fast a new customer reaches the Aha Moment) from lagging ones (renewal, a customer hitting their goal).

Chapter 16 adds the habit: after a feature ships, measure its effect on business drivers over time. A weekly scorecard is where that measurement lives. The workbook's line: if you're not tracking it, you're not managing it.

## Use it when

- The team can't say whether this week was better than last.
- The dashboard is full of green while churn or complaints rise.
- The headline metric is signups, users, or pageviews.
- Experiments are running but nobody checks their numbers on a cadence.
- After #15 Outcome Canvas, to put its North Star and inputs on a weekly rhythm.

## Before you start

Check which mode this is. **First run:** set up the North Star, target, and input metrics. **Later runs:** load last week's scorecard, move "This week" to "Last week," and fill the new week.

For a first run, the best input is the North Star and input metrics from #15 Outcome Canvas. If they don't have one, build it here, and tag it [Assumption] until they can show it moves with customer value. Ask whether they can actually pull each number weekly. A metric no one can pull gets tagged [Assumption] with "instrument it" as the action.

## Run it

**First run: Steps 1 to 5. Later runs: start at Step 4.**

**Step 1 of 7: North Star**
> What's the one number that rises only when your customers win?

Push for: delivered value, not activity. Probe: "Could this go up while customers fail?" If yes, keep looking. Run the actionable, accessible, auditable test out loud.

**Step 2 of 7: Target**
> What's the target for that number, and by when?

Push for: a specific value and date, grounded in where it is today.

**Step 3 of 7: Input metrics and owners**
> What three to five things can your team move that drive the North Star, and who owns each one?

Push for: inputs the team controls, each with one named owner and a target. Mix in at least one leading signal. Loop one metric at a time.

**Step 4 of 7: This week's numbers**
> What are this week's numbers for the North Star and each input?

Fill This week, carry Last week, and mark the trend (↑ ↓ →). For a first run, Last week can be blank or a best estimate tagged [Assumption].

**Step 5 of 7: Action if off**
> For anything off target or trending the wrong way, what's the action and who's doing it?

Push for: a real action with an owner, or a known reason. "Keep an eye on it" isn't an action.

**Step 6 of 7: Weekly health check**
> Five quick health checks, one at a time. First: is the North Star on track, or do you know why it's not?

Then ask the other four in turn: is at least one experiment in flight? Was customer feedback reviewed this week? Were churn or cancellation signals checked? Are next week's top three priorities agreed? Ask them one at a time, and ask for the evidence behind each yes: which experiment, which feedback, which churn signal. A yes without a specific gets marked unchecked.

**Step 7 of 7: Top three for next week**
> What are the top three priorities for next week?

Push for: three, tied to the metrics that are off. If none connect to the scorecard, ask why the scorecard isn't steering the week.

## Push back when

- **The North Star is a vanity metric.** "Signups go up when marketing works, even if the product fails. What rises only when customers win?"
- **Inputs have no owner.** "If everybody owns it, nobody does. Whose name goes on this?"
- **The scorecard is green and customers are leaving.** "A dashboard full of green can hide a product that isn't doing its job. What's churn saying?"
- **Health check boxes get ticked on faith.** "Which experiment? Name it, or the box stays empty."
- **Weeks get skipped.** "If you're not tracking it, you're not managing it. Ten minutes, same day every week."

```
[ ] Customer feedback was reviewed this week: [source]
[ ] Churn or cancellation signals were checked: [what you saw]
[ ] Top three priorities for next week are agreed: [1] [2] [3]
```

**Coaching notes**: 3 to 5 sentences on the week, for example whether the North Star passes the value test, which input is driving or dragging it, and which health check keeps coming back unchecked.

**Assumptions to test**: any [Assumption] metric, with what it takes to instrument it or prove it links to the North Star.

**Your next move this week**: one action, usually the owner and fix for the input furthest off target.

## Done well

- The North Star can't rise unless customers get value.
- Every input has one owner, a target, and a weekly number.
- Every health check yes names its evidence.
- Next week's priorities trace back to the scorecard.
