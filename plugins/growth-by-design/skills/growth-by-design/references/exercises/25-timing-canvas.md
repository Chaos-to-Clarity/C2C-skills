## Why it matters

When Bill Gross studied why startups succeed or fail, he expected the team or the idea to win. Timing came out on top, ahead of the team, the model, and the funding. Founders control it least and underrate it most, so read it honestly before betting years on it.

Every strong pitch deck answers why now. Sequoia bakes it into the "Why Now" slide: something shifted, whether a technology became feasible, a regulation changed, customer behavior moved, or a cost curve dropped. Too early and you're not going anywhere; too late and the wave is packed with surfers. Read readiness on three fronts: customer awareness of the problem, whether the technology can deliver now, and whether trend lines are climbing. Then place the market on the adoption lifecycle Everett Rogers mapped and Geoffrey Moore made famous; where your customers sit tells you how much education the sale needs.

Zoom spent years as a faster, more reliable video tool before 2020 sent the world home. Demand exploded overnight, and Zoom could capitalize because the product was already good enough to absorb the wave.

Luck favors the prepared. Your unfair advantage can't be easily copied: years of industry insight, a channel you already own, an asset nobody else has. Ash Maurya keeps a box for it on the Lean Canvas in Running Lean, and most founders leave it blank. Founder-market fit lives here too: if you can't say why this is your fight to win, someone else will. When the honest read is that timing isn't there, Eric Ries frames the call in The Lean Startup as pivot or persevere, and catalogs pivots by customer segment, problem, platform, or business model. If timing is what's wrong, you rarely need to abandon everything. Pivot to a different problem for the same customer, and keep your hardest-won asset: trust.

## Use it when

- The founder can't answer "why now?" in a sentence.
- Timing scored under 3, or Timing 4 (the specific shift) or 5 (growing demand) is a 1 or 2.
- Customers agree the problem exists but nobody is buying yet.
- Growth is flat and the team is debating whether to keep going.

## Before you start

Ask for demand evidence they can show: search trends, category growth, competitor traction (from #19 Competitive Intelligence Map), inbound interest. If they have none, the why-now is an [Assumption] and the first task is gathering one external and one customer signal.

The pivot question is heavy. Ask it plainly, without pushing toward an answer.

## Run it

**Step 1 of 7: The shift**
> What specifically changed, in technology, regulation, behavior, or cost, that makes this possible now and wasn't true five years ago?

Push for: one named shift with a date or source. "AI" alone is too broad; ask: "Which capability, made affordable when, and what does it unlock for your customer?"

**Step 2 of 7: Readiness on three fronts**
> Do your customers know they have this problem, can the technology deliver today, and are the trend lines climbing?

Ask the three one at a time. Push for: evidence on each. If customers aren't aware of the problem, name the cost: you'll be convincing people they have a problem.

**Step 3 of 7: Demand evidence**
> What data shows demand is real and growing, not flat?

Push for: search trends, category growth, or competitor traction with numbers and sources. Probe: "If a skeptical investor looked this up, what would they find?"

**Step 4 of 7: Adoption stage**
> Who's buying right now: innovators, early adopters, or the mainstream?

Push for: a stage tied to their actual customers, and what it means for education and patience in the sale.

**Step 5 of 7: Why-now in three sentences**
> Put it together: in three sentences, why can this win today when it couldn't five years ago?

Push for: the shift, the evidence, and the window. Draft it from Steps 1 to 4 if they stall, then let them edit.

**Step 6 of 7: Unfair advantage and founder-market fit**
> What do you have that a competitor can't easily copy, and why are you, specifically, the one to win this?

Push for: something real (an insight from years in the industry, a channel you own, a unique asset, a network). "Passion" and "we work hard" don't count. If blank, say so; most founders leave this box empty, and it's a real gap.

**Step 7 of 7: Pivot, persevere, or kill**
> Looking at all of this honestly, do you persevere, pivot, or stop, and what signal is driving that call?

Push for: a decision tied to a signal (flat demand, stalled growth, customers ghosting, or the opposite). If pivot, ask which kind: segment, problem, platform, or business model. If timing is the problem, raise the book's option: a different problem for the same customer.

## Push back when

- **The why-now is a trend headline.** "Lots of people see that trend. What specifically does it make possible for your customer?"
- **No demand data.** "Is demand growing, or do you hope it is? Show me one number."
- **The unfair advantage is effort.** "Anyone can work hard. What can't be copied?"
- **They're selling mainstream to early adopters, or the reverse.** "Where are your actual buyers on the curve? Sell to them."
- **They want to walk away from customers who trust them.** "If timing is what's wrong, pivot to a different problem for the same customer. Their trust is your hardest-won asset."
- **They refuse to consider a pivot despite flat signals.** "What would have to happen for you to change course? Write it down now."

```
Adoption stage of current buyers: [innovators / early adopters / mainstream]  [Evidence/Assumption]
Demand evidence (search trends, category growth, competitor traction): [data + sources]  [Evidence/Assumption]
Your unfair advantage (what a competitor can't easily copy): [advantage]  [Evidence/Assumption]
Founder-market fit (why you, specifically): [experience, network, insight]  [Evidence/Assumption]
Pivot, persevere, or kill (the call, and the signal driving it): [call] · [signal] · [pivot type if any]  [Evidence/Assumption]
```

**Coaching notes**: 3 to 5 sentences on what the canvas revealed: whether the shift is specific, whether demand data exists, whether the unfair advantage is real, whether the call follows the signals.

**Assumptions to test**: each [Assumption] field with the cheapest test (a search trend check, a competitor traction scan, or asking five customers whether this problem got worse recently).

**Your next move this week**: one concrete action, usually "gather one external demand signal and one customer signal, and revisit the call."

## Done well

- The shift is specific and dated.
- Demand evidence includes at least one number with a source.
- The unfair advantage would survive a competitor's attempt to copy it.
- The pivot, persevere, or kill call names the signal behind it.
