# PRODUCT DISCOVERY FRAMEWORK
# "DON’T SUCK, BE AWESOME"
Author: Eric Weiss  
Purpose: Emotion-Driven Product-Market Fit Discovery  
Audience: Founders, Product Leaders, Early-Stage SaaS / AI Teams  

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS FRAMEWORK

Most product discovery fails because teams ask polite questions.

They ask:
- “Would you use this?”
- “Is this interesting?”
- “Does this seem helpful?”

Customers say yes.
Then they do not buy.

This framework surfaces:

- Emotional frustration
- Identity friction
- Unmet desire
- Energy leaks
- Real purchasing drivers

It focuses on:

What sucks → What would be awesome instead

The product must eliminate pain and amplify aspiration.

---

# 2. CORE PHILOSOPHY

People do not buy features.

They buy:

- Relief
- Speed
- Status
- Control
- Confidence
- Identity alignment

Your job is to uncover:

Where they feel friction.
Where they feel stupid.
Where they feel slow.
Where they feel anxious.
Where they feel invisible.

Then design against that.

---

# 3. WHEN TO USE THIS

Use when:

- Exploring new product idea
- Repositioning product
- Stalled traction
- High churn
- Weak messaging
- Fundraising narrative unclear

Do not use for:

- Validating solution features prematurely
- Running usability tests
- Internal brainstorming

This is for emotional discovery.

---

# 4. PHASE 1 — WHAT SUCKS?

Goal:
Surface emotional frustration.

Rules:

- Ask open-ended questions.
- Avoid leading language.
- Capture verbatim quotes.
- Interrupt vagueness.
- Do not pitch your solution.

Core Questions:

- Walk me through the last time you dealt with this.
- What pissed you off about that?
- Where did it waste your time?
- When did you feel stuck?
- What part makes you want to avoid it?
- What do you dread most about this process?
- What do you complain about to colleagues?
- What feels fragile or unreliable?

Follow-ups:

- Why is that frustrating?
- What happens if that continues?
- What does that cost you?
- How does that make you look to your team?
- What do you do as a workaround?

Listen for:

- Strong emotional language
- Repeated phrases
- Workarounds
- Manual hacks
- Avoidance behaviors

Output:

List of core pain statements in customer’s words.

---

# 5. PHASE 2 — WHAT’S AWESOME?

Goal:
Surface aspiration.

Core Questions:

- What do you like about your current solution?
- What works surprisingly well?
- What would make this effortless?
- What would feel magical?
- What would make you proud to use this?
- If this worked perfectly, what would change?

Push deeper:

- What would that free you to focus on?
- What would that make you capable of?
- What would your team say about you?
- What stress would disappear?

Output:

List of desired emotional states.

---

# 6. PHASE 3 — POINT VS COUNTERPOINT

Now convert pain into transformation.

Template:

Instead of:
[Specific suck]

Wouldn’t it be awesome if:
[Specific transformation]

Examples:

Instead of:
“I have to manually reconcile three dashboards.”

Wouldn’t it be awesome if:
“I had one live view that just told me what mattered.”

Instead of:
“I have to double-check everything my team does.”

Wouldn’t it be awesome if:
“I trusted outputs without hovering.”

Apply this across:

- Workflow friction
- Tool fragmentation
- Identity stress
- Team dynamics
- Status insecurity

---

# 7. STACK THE CONTRASTS

Do not stop at surface-level friction.

Ask:

- What sucks about that?
- What sucks about that?
- What sucks about that?

Keep drilling until you reach:

- Identity threat
- Status risk
- Career risk
- Emotional exhaustion

Most products solve surface pain.
Breakthrough products solve identity friction.

---

# 8. PERSONA APPLICATION

This framework works best when grounded in vivid personas.

Example Persona 1: Project Manager

Pain:
- Juggling too many dependencies
- Constant Slack interruptions
- No visibility into progress
- Fear of missing something

Aspiration:
- Calm control
- Clear prioritization
- Visible accountability
- Respect from team

Example Persona 2: Engineering Leader

Pain:
- Feature creep
- Rewriting code
- Endless edge cases
- Sales pressure

Aspiration:
- Stable architecture
- Clear roadmap
- Predictable velocity
- Technical credibility

The emotional language must be specific.

---

# 9. WHAT NOT TO DO

Avoid:

- Asking about hypothetical future use
- Showing wireframes too early
- Pitching your idea
- Defending your assumptions
- Over-validating minor problems
- Accepting weak emotional signals

If no strong language appears:
The problem is weak.

---

# 10. MESSAGE EXTRACTION

After interviews, extract:

- Exact phrasing
- Emotional triggers
- Repeated themes
- Identity desires

These become:

- Headline copy
- Landing page language
- Sales scripts
- Investor narrative

Never rewrite customer language into corporate language.

---

# 11. SIGNALS OF REAL PROBLEM

Strong problem signals include:

- Workarounds
- Spreadsheets replacing tools
- Manual reconciliation
- Emotional irritation
- Money already being spent
- Time already being wasted
- Active complaining

Weak signals:

- Mild inconvenience
- Hypothetical interest
- Polite encouragement
- Low urgency

---

# 12. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES

If another AI uses this framework:

It must:

1. Ask one question at a time.
2. Avoid leading the witness.
3. Capture language verbatim.
4. Interrupt vagueness.
5. Push for emotional specificity.
6. Separate pain from aspiration.
7. Convert into structured contrasts.
8. Refuse to jump to solution prematurely.

Tone:

- Curious
- Calm
- Direct
- Slightly confrontational when answers are vague
- Focused on reality

Never:

- Praise ideas
- Validate without evidence
- Skip emotional layer
- Collapse pain and aspiration together
