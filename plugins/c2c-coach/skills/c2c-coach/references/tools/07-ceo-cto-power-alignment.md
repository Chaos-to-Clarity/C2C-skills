# CEO / CTO POWER ALIGNMENT MODEL
Author: Eric Weiss  
Purpose: Executive Authority Redesign & Power Transition Management  
Audience: Founder-CEO and Technical Co-Founder / CTO  

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS FRAMEWORK

As companies scale, founder dynamics shift.

Common scenario:

- CEO built the company.
- CTO was early employee or technical co-founder.
- CEO previously made all final decisions.
- CTO now has title authority but not real autonomy.
- Power feels ambiguous.
- Disagreements escalate emotionally.
- Loudest voice wins.

This model:

- Clarifies authority
- Defines decision domains
- Reduces political behavior
- Enables productive disagreement
- Protects company from ego-driven conflict

This is not about friendship.
This is about structural power clarity.

---

# 2. WHY POWER TENSIONS EMERGE

Power transitions trigger identity threats.

CEO fears:
- Loss of control
- Quality decline
- Slower execution
- Irrelevance

CTO fears:
- Undermining
- Public override
- Lack of trust
- Title without authority

When unaddressed:

- Decisions stall
- Team triangulates
- Back-channel influence grows
- Respect erodes
- Execution slows

Power ambiguity creates organizational instability.

---

# 3. PRINCIPLE 1 — EXPLICIT AUTHORITY MAP

Define three categories:

1. CEO-Exclusive Decisions
2. CTO-Exclusive Decisions
3. Joint Decisions

Template:

| Decision Category | Owner | Consultation Required | Tie-Break Rule |
|------------------|-------|----------------------|----------------|

Rules:

- No undefined shared authority.
- No invisible veto power.
- No public overrides without private discussion.

If escalation rules are unclear:
Conflict becomes personal.

---

# 4. PRINCIPLE 2 — DEFINE TECHNICAL OWNERSHIP BOUNDARIES

Clarify:

- Who defines architecture?
- Who approves refactors?
- Who decides build vs buy?
- Who owns technical debt prioritization?
- Who defines performance standards?

CEO input is strategic.
CTO authority is operational.

Without boundary clarity:
Every disagreement becomes existential.

---

# 5. PRINCIPLE 3 — PRODUCT VS PLATFORM DISTINCTION

Many CEO/CTO conflicts are miscategorized.

Clarify:

- Product roadmap ownership
- Technical feasibility authority
- Platform stability prioritization
- Delivery timelines

Define:

- Who sets customer promise?
- Who sets technical constraints?
- How conflicts are resolved?

Never allow product pressure to override platform stability without explicit agreement.

---

# 6. PRINCIPLE 4 — DISAGREEMENT PROTOCOL

High-performing exec teams disagree directly.

Define:

- Where disagreements are discussed
- Time limit on unresolved decisions
- Data required before debate
- Post-decision alignment rule

Post-decision rule:

Once decision is made:
Both publicly support it.

No silent sabotage.
No passive resistance.

---

# 7. PRINCIPLE 5 — IDENTITY SHIFT

CEO must transition from:

Builder → Architect

CTO must transition from:

Implementer → Executive

CEO emotional shifts:

- Trust over control
- Standards over interference
- Leverage over speed

CTO emotional shifts:

- Ownership over defensiveness
- Clarity over ambiguity
- Accountability over technical purity

Without identity shift:
Structural clarity fails.

---

# 8. POWER RESET SESSION STRUCTURE

Use this structure in live alignment:

Step 1:
Each executive defines:

- What decisions they believe they own.
- Where they feel overridden.
- Where they feel unclear.

Step 2:
List conflicts explicitly.

Step 3:
Define authority map in writing.

Step 4:
Define escalation and tie-break rules.

Step 5:
Commit publicly to post-decision alignment rule.

Document it.

Revisit quarterly.

---

# 9. FAILURE MODES

AI must guard against:

- Founder claiming delegation without real authority transfer.
- CTO avoiding accountability behind technical complexity.
- Shared ownership without tie-breaker.
- Emotional arguments disguised as strategy.
- Public override behavior.
- Avoidance of direct conflict.

Power must be explicit or politics fills the gap.

---

# 10. ORGANIZATIONAL SIGNALS OF MISALIGNMENT

- Team asks CEO for technical decisions.
- CTO waits for CEO validation.
- Roadmap changes weekly.
- Engineering morale drops.
- Meetings increase.
- Silent resentment builds.

Signal of alignment:

- Decisions happen without drama.
- Authority respected publicly.
- Disagreements resolved quickly.
- Team understands who owns what.

---

# 11. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES

If another AI uses this framework:

It must:

1. Force explicit authority categories.
2. Expose invisible veto behaviors.
3. Push for documented decision matrix.
4. Require post-decision alignment rule.
5. Surface emotional identity fears.
6. Avoid neutral conflict facilitation language.
7. End with structural commitments.

Tone:

- Direct
- Calm
- Uncomfortable but constructive
- Authority-focused
- Reality-based

Never:

- Frame conflict as personality mismatch.
- Allow vague shared ownership.
- Accept “we’ll figure it out.”
- Skip identity transition discussion.
