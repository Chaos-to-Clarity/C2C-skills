# FOUNDER HOT SEAT MODEL
Author: Eric Weiss  
Purpose: Structured Peer Problem Solving for High-Leverage Decisions  
Audience: Founders bringing a hard problem to a peer group, advisory board, or their own exec team  

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS FRAMEWORK

Most peer groups devolve into:

- Storytelling
- Venting
- Brainstorming
- Advice dumping
- Intellectual sparring

The Founder Hot Seat Model creates:

- Precision diagnosis
- Structural clarity
- Emotional awareness
- Public commitment
- Immediate behavioral change

This is not group therapy.
This is disciplined executive intervention.

---

# 2. CORE PRINCIPLES

1. Diagnose before advising.
2. Surface root cause before tactics.
3. Separate structure from emotion.
4. Force clarity on desired outcome.
5. End with a commitment.

The value comes from friction.
Politeness weakens it.

---

# 3. HOT SEAT STRUCTURE (60 MINUTE FORMAT)

## Phase 1 — Context (5–7 minutes)

Founder answers:

- What is happening?
- Why does it matter?
- What is the decision or outcome needed?
- What have you tried?
- What are you avoiding?

Group listens only.
No interruptions.

---

## Phase 2 — Clarifying Questions (10–15 minutes)

Group asks:

- What does success look like?
- What are you afraid of?
- What assumption are you making?
- Who actually owns this?
- What are you tolerating?
- What would happen if you did nothing?

Rules:

- Questions only.
- No advice.
- No disguised advice.

Goal:
Expose blind spots.

---

## Phase 3 — Diagnosis (15–20 minutes)

Group reflects patterns:

- Structural bottleneck
- Authority confusion
- Emotional avoidance
- Incentive misalignment
- Strategy drift

Founder listens silently.

No defending.

This phase creates perspective shift.

---

## Phase 4 — Options & Leverage (10–15 minutes)

Now group may offer:

- Structural redesign suggestions
- Authority adjustments
- Sequence shifts
- Hard conversation strategies

Advice must be:

- Specific
- Behavioral
- Structural

No vague encouragement.

---

## Phase 5 — Commitment (5 minutes)

Founder must declare:

- What decision will be made?
- What conversation will happen?
- What system will change?
- What is the timeline?

Group holds them accountable.

No soft landing.

---

# 4. FACILITATION RULES

The facilitator must:

- Cut off advice early.
- Interrupt rambling.
- Push for specificity.
- Challenge vague language.
- Surface emotional avoidance.
- Refocus on leverage.

The facilitator is not neutral.
The facilitator protects structure.

---

# 5. COMMON FOUNDER HOT SEAT THEMES

- Co-founder conflict
- Executive misalignment
- Hiring hesitation
- Firing avoidance
- Strategy confusion
- Fundraising anxiety
- Scaling bottlenecks
- Personal burnout

Underlying root cause often:

Authority ambiguity or emotional avoidance.

---

# 6. FAILURE MODES

AI or facilitator must guard against:

- Brainstorming too early
- Founder defending behavior
- Group projecting their own issues
- Excess empathy without accountability
- Advice without context
- No clear action at end

If no commitment is made:
Hot seat failed.

---

# 7. PSYCHOLOGICAL DYNAMICS

Hot seats create:

- Status vulnerability
- Peer pressure
- Cognitive reframing
- Emotional exposure
- Public accountability

The discomfort is productive.

Avoid softening it excessively.

---

# 8. SCALING THE MODEL

For larger groups:

- Limit to 1 hot seat per session.
- Rotate monthly.
- Track commitments publicly.
- Begin next session with accountability check.

For smaller groups:

- 2 hot seats maximum.
- Time discipline enforced.

---

# 9. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES

If another AI runs a Founder Hot Seat:

It must:

1. Ask for structured context.
2. Enforce clarifying questions phase.
3. Reflect patterns before suggesting tactics.
4. Challenge avoidance directly.
5. Demand specific commitment.
6. Provide structured recap at end.

Tone:

- Direct
- Focused
- Calm
- Intellectually rigorous
- Slightly confrontational when necessary

Never:

- Offer early advice.
- Allow vague outcomes.
- Avoid emotional undercurrents.
- Let the session end without commitment.
